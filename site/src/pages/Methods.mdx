# Methods

We used a sequence of complementary methods to describe, cluster, and forecast housing markets while incorporating demographics.

### 1) Data merging and scope
- Sources: Zillow HPI (monthly state-level median home prices) and CPS/IPUMS (annual demographics, income, property tax) for 51 regions (50 states + DC).
- Cleaning: housing gaps were linearly interpolated; early missing stretches (e.g., North Dakota) were filled with first available value and flagged.
- CPS interpolation: demographic features were interpolated to monthly cadence. We focused on major race categories (White, Black, Asian, Native American) due to sparsity in finer combinations.
- Train/test split: 80/20 (train through Jan 2016, test Feb 2016–Jan 2020). Post-2020 excluded due to COVID-era shocks.

### 2) Classical decomposition and detrending
- Evaluated differencing, moving average, and OLS detrending; differencing produced the most stationary series for downstream modeling.
- Seasonal pattern: many states showed double-peaked seasonality within a year; residuals became erratic post-2020, justifying the pre-2020 forecasting window.

### 3) Time-series K-means clustering
- Clustered by percentage change in prices (2–20 clusters). Central states grouped together consistently; coastal states (e.g., CA, FL) often formed small distinct clusters.
- Practical implication: geographic diversification can be approximated by selecting states from distinct clusters rather than neighboring regions.
- Diminishing returns: beyond ~7 clusters, singleton groups proliferate, reducing interpretability.

### 4) Forecasting models
- Univariate ARIMA (auto_arima): searched p,q up to 12; best typical fit ARIMA(4,1,0). Forecasts tended to extend recent linear trends; accuracy varied widely across states with sometimes large predictive intervals.
- VARMAX on clustered states: fitting per-cluster multivariate models improved trend tracking versus univariate ARIMA when clusters were non-trivial. VARMAX with demographic covariates often failed to converge and underperformed.

### 5) Bayesian hierarchical modeling
- Linear model with state-specific intercepts/slopes and global coefficients for tax, population, and race proportions. Predictors standardized (z-scores) before sampling.
- Priors: weakly informative normals; hierarchical priors on state intercept/slope (hyperparameters $\mu, \eta$) with exponential priors on their scales; Half-Cauchy noise scale.
- Inference: PyMC3 NUTS with 4 chains, 2k tune + 2k draws per chain (8k total samples).
- Outputs: posterior intervals for each state’s slope/intercept (growth level and rate) and global covariate effects; trace plots indicate good mixing.
